## D. The three-metre test

**Selected frame:** `references/sections/B-cold-industrial/03-snow-ropeway.jpg`. It provides the clearest pedestrian foreground with surface divisions, edge construction, façades and signs together. It is a descending-stair view, not a calibrated photograph taken exactly three metres from each subject.

| Distinct visible thing | Material read and close-focus requirement |
|---|---|
| **Broad stair treads and risers** | Heavy mineral slabs **inferred**; concrete versus stone unresolved. Each tread has a readable front edge, uneven pale coverage and darker exposed areas. |
| **Joints and edge irregularities** | Dark separations interrupt the pale stair field. Edges are worn-looking and nonuniform; exact chipped construction is uncertain. |
| **Pale surface deposits** | Snow/frost-like accumulation, also visible on roofs. Coverage varies across treads rather than forming identical stripes. |
| **Handrails, posts and brackets** | Dark metal-like members with occasional rusty-red colour. Main supports, horizontal rails and thinner infill have distinct thicknesses. |
| **Façade surfaces and ledges** | Blue-grey, rough, unevenly coloured mineral-looking walls with dark recesses and projecting edges. Exact wall substrate unresolved. |
| **Windows and guards** | Dark frames, glass-like panes, grid divisions and exterior metal-like guards. Lit panes remain distinct from the surrounding wall. |
| **Projecting sign panels** | Thin framed objects with luminous faces, warm yellow/orange/red colour and dark attachment arms. Text is not reliably readable. |
| **Overhanging roofs / shelters** | Thin sheet-like surfaces, some covered by pale deposits, with clear projecting edges and supporting members. Exact sheet material unresolved. |
| **Balconies and exterior framework** | Railings, narrow platforms and struts visibly attach to fronts. They do not float independently of the architecture. |
| **Cables and roof poles** | Thin dark runs with sag and crossing directions, plus taller rigid pole/frame elements. |
| **People** | Two prominent dark clothed figures on the stairs and many smaller figures below. Garment shapes read; fine fabric, accessories and faces do not. |

**Close-focus bar:** At three metres, the viewer must distinguish **surface, joint, edge, support and attachment**. Fine grain alone does not meet that bar. Human-scale construction estimates, not reference measurements: handrails around **0.9–1.1 m** high; step rises around **0.15–0.20 m**.

## E. What the supplied output is missing

`01b-crowd-closeup.png` and `01c-street-crowd.png` belong to **B** in `references/sections.json`; the following comparison uses B’s references.

1. **A legible pedestrian surface and boundary.** Roughly half each supplied frame is blue-grey ground. Raised crossing bands exist, but do not establish a convincing footway, threshold or retaining edge. B’s stairs supply repeated tread faces, joints and rail-defined boundaries.
2. **Continuous inhabited frontage beside the route.** The supplied views have isolated pale block forms and enormous open foreground panels. B has fronts, ledges, entrances, projecting signs and shelters directly adjoining its pedestrian circulation.
3. **Projecting illuminated objects at human scale.** B’s warm sign faces and small apertures sit against cold façades and make the corridor readable. The supplied foreground has no comparable sign silhouettes, brackets or local light accents.
4. **Railings, posts and attached edge structures.** These establish scale and perspective in B. The supplied strips mostly remain horizontal ground divisions.
5. **Material variation tied to construction.** B has dark seams, irregular pale deposits, exposed patches and uneven edges. Our panels vary mainly through lighting and shadows; their surfaces provide almost no additional information on approach.
6. **Visible people in these particular frames.** The references show figures on the near stairs and throughout the distant corridor. Neither supplied street shot has clearly legible pedestrians. This observation does not establish whether pedestrians render elsewhere.

The contact sheet shows that the large skyline already carries extensive detail. The principal gap is the **lowest few metres and the walking surface**, where objects need identifiable functions and attachments.

## F. One risk

> **Superseded in part by [DECISIONS D30](DECISIONS.md) (owner, 2026-10-13):** furniture
> now also stands inside wide walking areas, as islands people use, keeping a clear route
> of at least ~3 m. The risk below (uniform repetition) and the irregular, clumped
> placement still apply; "cluster furniture beside fronts and route boundaries" no longer
> limits where furniture may go.

**Uniform procedural repetition will turn the street into another noisy pattern.** Identical cracks, evenly spaced props and repeated canopy modules can fill the empty ground while destroying the references’ large value groups and section differences.

Spend scarce geometry on **edges, thickness, posts, brackets and canopy silhouettes**. Put joints and restrained wear into procedural shading. Cluster furniture beside fronts and route boundaries, leaving coherent walking areas. Preserve A’s warm/cool planes, B’s cold surfaces with small warm accents, C’s dark foreground against lit galleries, and D’s timber/concrete/plant/water separation.
