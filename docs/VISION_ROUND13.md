dark strip is what the eye takes first. The blue edge reads as its rim. I
cannot follow a clearly higher pavement into a kerb top, down its exposed
face and onto a lower road. Instead, similar grey planes surround a dark
linear interruption. That is the gutter/channel reading: a dark slot bordered
by an edge, rather than a supported walking surface ending above the road.
This is a judgement of the photographed relationship, not a claim that the
emitted face is absent or that its height is wrong.

**The contrast is too isolating in this composition.** The edge becomes a
near-black graphic on an otherwise quiet grey plane. It does not read as a
step too tall to walk beside; it reads as a conspicuous channel. More contrast
would reinforce that identity. The unresolved issue is which value belongs
to the raised top, the descending face and the adjoining ground, rather than
an overall brightness target.

In B's snow-ropeway frame, pale treads, dark fronts and broken edges belong to
a continuous descending surface. The viewer understands above and below
before inspecting the joints. Our profile now has a visible edge, but the
surfaces around it do not establish that same physical relationship.
