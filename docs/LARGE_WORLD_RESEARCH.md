# Rich city, continuous streaming, and 60 FPS

Research date: **2026-10-06**. Implementation status: **candidates only**.
Owner target: **this Mac, Google Chrome, 1600 × 900**. The parent agent verified
the machine as **Mac14,15, Apple M2, 16 GB**. No performance run or visual
capture was performed for this research note.

This work follows the existing six-item ground/kerb/crowd/market review batch.
It does not replace that batch or claim the city meets the performance target.
Fortnite is developed by **Epic Games**, rather than EA.

## Recommendation

Adapt the published principles: load essential representations first, retain
cheap distant geometry, spend detail where it becomes visible, and keep work
off the frame's critical path. Start with the measured bottleneck, preserve the
existing renderer, and migrate one layer at a time. Importing Unreal Engine,
implementing all of Nanite, or reproducing a console asset pipeline is not a
prerequisite for any of these improvements.

The strongest initial candidate is a **worker-backed, prioritized generation
and bake queue**, followed by **separate coarse skyline and near-detail
residency**. These answer two concrete source-level limitations below. Their
performance benefit still requires a controlled Chrome benchmark.

## What the published games actually demonstrate

### Insomniac: original Spider-Man

**S1. Elan Ruskin, “Marvel’s Spider-Man: A Technical Postmortem”, GDC,
March 2019.** [Official GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026496/-Marvel-s-Spider-Man).
The [public speaker slide PDF](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/8luzlixaz0j4ih2ba3l4k/ElanRuskin_gdc2019_marvels_spider-man_preliminaryexport.pdf?rlkey=8bgy3q8az0x8bth8lnzan63j1&dl=1)
was downloaded and its actual contents inspected; page numbers below are
one-based PDF pages, including repeated animation slides. Its filename labels
it a preliminary export, so these are published presentation claims, not a
complete description of the final engine.

- Pages 119–122 derive tile deadlines from traversal: 32 m/s, 128 m tiles,
  roughly a 3 × 3 local set, and forward-region priority. The shown 0.8–1.33 s
  figures are workload calculations, not Syzygy targets.
- Pages 129–133 divide loading into essential data and delayed detail; page 46
  shows asynchronous decompression outside frame synchronization.
- Pages 182–195 describe autogenerated low-polygon **3D** distant objects with
  shared zone atlases. Pages 198–204 retain silhouette-changing rooftop objects
  as compact hibernated models and hydrate richer instances locally.
- Pages 40–45 show background LOD swaps and occlusion work in the frame schedule.

For this project, substitute deterministic generation and procedural material
evaluation for disk asset/texture streaming. Preserve the separation of
representations and scheduling; the source's atlases and disk budgets do not
transfer directly. [S1](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026496/-Marvel-s-Spider-Man)

**S2. David Santiago, “Procedural System Assisted Authoring of Open-World
Content for Marvel’s Spider-Man”, SIGGRAPH ’19 Talks, July 28–August 1, 2019.**
[Author paper hosted by SIGGRAPH](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2019-Talks-Santiago_Procedural-System-Assisted-Authoring.pdf),

### Epic: demonstrated Fortnite features

**S6. Graham Wihlidal, “Bringing Nanite to Fortnite Battle Royale in Chapter
4”, Epic Games, January 26, 2023.**
[Epic engineering article](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4).
It documents Nanite building meshes; offline displacement to create geometry;
avoiding masked foliage where opaque geometry performed better; foliage area
preservation during simplification; and disabling distant wind deformation.
Its useful lessons are to retain visible geometric/foliage coverage and stop
evaluating detail that cannot be resolved. The foliage result belongs to Nanite's
particular rasterizer and must be benchmarked in this WebGPU renderer. Offline
displacement textures conflict with this repository's no-texture-assets decision.

**S7. Andrew Lauritzen and Ola Olsson, “Virtual Shadow Maps in Fortnite
Battle Royale Chapter 4”, Epic Games, January 26, 2023.**
[Epic engineering article](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/virtual-shadow-maps-in-fortnite-battle-royale-chapter-4).
It reports that moving sun direction and animated trees undermine shadow-page
caching. It also describes small-object filtering, distance-limited deformation,
shadow proxy evaluation, and effective caching for static local lights.
The direct lesson is to budget shadow work independently and account for
invalidation. A universal cache or far-shadow shortcut is not automatically
correct; blindly skipping updates caused visible artifacts in their experiments.

### Epic: general Unreal/UEFN features, not proof of every Fortnite subsystem

These are current, first-party documentation pages checked on 2026-10-06.
They do not display a publication date; Unreal pages resolved to version 5.8.

**S8. “Streaming and HLODs”, Unreal Editor for Fortnite documentation.**
[UEFN docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/streaming-and-hlods-in-unreal-editor-for-fortnite).
World Partition streams cells near a source; HLOD preserves distant grouped
representations when detailed cells unload. HLODs add storage/memory, so streaming
alone and streaming plus HLOD have different budgets. This is an official UEFN
workflow, not a full account of Battle Royale's production streaming system.

**S9. “World Partition”, Unreal Engine documentation.**
[UE docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine).
Streaming sources, spatial loading, source priorities, and configurable ranges
separate world organization from residency. Additional sources can preload a
destination. This supports camera-plus-predicted-position demand in Syzygy;
unannounced teleportation still requires either a fallback or preparation time.

**S10. “World Partition - Hierarchical Level of Detail”, Unreal Engine
documentation.**
[UE HLOD docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine).
HLOD layers support instancing, merged meshes, simplified meshes, and their own
cell sizes/ranges. Proxies represent unloaded fine cells. A procedural,
vertex-colored skyline proxy fits our material policy; copying texture-atlas
baking does not.

**S11. “Using PCG Generation Modes”, Unreal Engine documentation.**
[UE PCG docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine).
Hierarchical generation uses coarser grids for prominent objects and finer grids
for local detail. Runtime scheduling considers distance and view direction,
separate generation/cleanup ranges reduce sudden changes, and pooling limits
allocation churn. This is a general UE feature; this note does not claim
Fortnite's shipped city placement uses this exact PCG system.

**S12. “Nanite Virtualized Geometry”, Unreal Engine documentation.**
[UE Nanite docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine).
Nanite builds hierarchical triangle clusters, selects detail with the camera
view, and streams required geometry. It is a specialized rendering/data system.
Screen-dependent detail and finer visibility units can be adapted incrementally;
using those ideas does not make our renderer Nanite.

These are source observations, not current timing measurements. Existing timing
comments remain historical observations until repeated on the agreed target.

| `src/render/renderer.ts`, `Renderer.create`, constructs `City(defaultCityConfig(seed))`; `src/world/citygen.ts`, `defaultCityConfig`, sets `chunkSize: 256`, `radiusChunks: 6`. `City.getChunk` rejects coordinates outside that radius. Search found no runtime radius override. | Streaming is currently bounded. Do not claim the current runtime already streams forever. Radius also determines CPU cache capacity, coupling world extent and residency. |
| `City.getChunk` packs `(cx + 4096) * 8192 + (cz + 4096)` into a numeric key. | `Renderer.streamChunks` performs `City.getChunk`, `bakeChunk`, and `uploadChunk` synchronously. The budget is tested between whole jobs; the comment explicitly acknowledges that an atomic bake can exceed 4 ms. | A time-budgeted loop cannot preempt a costly bake. Worker execution or resumable micro-jobs are candidates; changing the budget constant alone cannot guarantee 16.67 ms. |
| Missing-job priority is built only when that queue empties. Rebakes drain before missing chunks. Both lists sort by chunk distance, without velocity, view direction, or job deadline. | Rapid movement or a tier transition can leave outdated work ahead of newly urgent visible coverage. |
| `src/render/quality.ts`, `lodForDistance`, is called for initial bakes and moving-camera rebakes. High caps at LOD 1; ultra forces LOD 0. LOD uses camera chunk distance, without transition hysteresis. | LOD is live. D7's statement that every chunk is baked at LOD 0 is stale. Optimize the existing path; watch repeated boundary rebakes and changes in distant identity. |
| `src/render/citymesh.ts`, `bakeChunk`, consumes a shared seeded RNG through LOD-dependent branches; props are probabilistically omitted. | Before caching several representations, prove that the same building's identifying silhouette/material seed survives LOD changes. Independent feature seeds are a candidate, not a confirmed defect fix. |
| Baked chunk meshes combine buildings, props, bridges, cables, and ground detail. G-buffer and cascades cull chunk bounding spheres. `bakeGround(4096, seed)` builds a finite shared ground mesh. | City detail can require a large rebuild; a visible sliver can submit a whole chunk. | Renderer limits frames in flight using queue completion promises. Stats derive frame time from intervals between successful render publications; `world.perf.gpuMs = frameMs - cpuEncodeMs`. The device requests `timestamp-query` when available, but source search found no query writes. | Existing FPS is neither direct display presentation telemetry nor a GPU timestamp. Queue completion, submission count, rAF callbacks, CPU stages, and GPU pass timings must remain distinct. |
| `App.warmChunks` synchronously calls the app loop before/after pose setup; capture warm-up can fill residency. | A warm static capture cannot demonstrate hitch-free traversal or cold startup. Keep it for visual evidence and benchmark the continuous cold path separately. |

## What would substantiate 60 FPS and no traversal loading screens

The numerical frame budget is **1000 / 60 = 16.67 ms**. Record raw samples and
p50/p95/p99/max; an average can conceal streaming hitches. Count callbacks,
submissions, completions, skipped callbacks, and outstanding work separately.
Sample GPU query results asynchronously so the measurement does not fence every
frame. Optional timestamp support must fail gracefully. CPU/GPU overlap means
their times cannot simply be subtracted from an interval to obtain GPU duration.

The parent agent verified the available browser as **Google Chrome for Testing**
in Playwright's `chromium-1243` cache; a stable Chrome application was not found
in `/Applications`. Use that installed Chrome build without installing another
browser, and disclose its exact version, headed/headless mode and WebGPU adapter.
Do not describe it as an installed stable-channel Chrome application. Measure at
1600 × 900 output and disclose
actual internal render resolution. Run a fixed-quality control and an adaptive
run; a quality governor silently lowering fidelity is not evidence for the
fixed-quality claim. Preserve identical scene seed, route, weather and settings
between controls.

The benchmark route should include a dense ground-level market, high aerial
skyline, rapid forward flight across generation boundaries, sharp reversal,
district changes, a long outward/return trip, and controlled tier transitions.
Check memory plateaus and geometry coverage after eviction. Run one GPU workload
at a time on this machine. Screenshots prove visual continuity; raw timing and
queue data prove performance. Neither substitutes for the other.

“Zero loading time” should mean **continuous traversal without a blocking loading
screen, holes, or disruptive stalls**. The first page load still performs code
download, device/pipeline setup and initial generation; report its time to first
useful frame separately. An arbitrary teleport to an unprepared location cannot
have complete detailed data ready by definition. Preload announced destinations
or show a valid coarse representation while fine detail becomes ready; do not
claim that deferred work has vanished.

No performance acceptance threshold beyond the owner's 60 FPS target is being
declared achieved here. Select the final frame-pacing tolerance from the real
benchmark and its display refresh, and record any unavoidable misses honestly.
