## The discriminating test now binds exposed surfaces

The controlled-scene and same-subject requirements were already in
[HERO_FRAME_SPEC.md](HERO_FRAME_SPEC.md). I retained them and made the ground
relationship explicit: bind the selected counter's actual front and end,
support, adjoining walking surface and roadward transition, near occupants,
occupied middle frontage and exit opening before changing phase. The same
camera and saved actor/geometry state must serve both members; lens and
weather remain shared. The only change is phase and its normal authored
lighting response.

**Daylight must expose construction and occupation together.** The selected
near counter has a working front and support visibly meeting the ground.
Cloth, counter structure and rigging have distinct material reads. The
adjoining surface reads as a place to walk, with a physical boundary to the
street and independent people visibly standing on it. Passage beside
counter-facing occupation continues through the middle frontage to the exit.
A hatch, paint line or visible body inventory does not supply any missing
relationship.

**Night must reveal those same relationships through received light.** The
eye first finds market activity through warm pavement, a working counter
front and grounded human silhouettes. It then follows occupation beside
successive frontages to the same opening. Dark pauses can group businesses;
they cannot erase near support-to-ground or body-to-ground relationships,
flatten people into posts, or break the middle passage. Upper-city detail
and fine surface pattern may recede. The near subjects retain the human scale
that makes the light useful.

The reviewer reads each member independently, then compares the same named
subjects. The findings must distinguish:

| Evidence at the corresponding subject | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Daylight exposes supported frontage, a readable walking surface, independent grounded occupants and occupied continuation; night preserves them while received light directs attention. | The street is resolved and the light is doing its job. |
| Daylight demonstrates a ribbon, marker crowd, unsupported frontage or broken passage; night hides that same defect in darkness or bloom. | Darkness is hiding an unresolved street. Name the defect and what conceals it. Night cannot approve it. |
| Night reveals a floating support, marker silhouette or lost ground contact that daylight made easy to overlook. | The street is unresolved. The revealing light supplies evidence, rather than something to soften away. |
| Daylight establishes the relationships; night loses the corresponding counter, grounded occupants or route. | Night lighting fails. That does not alone prove a shared geometry fault. |
| A required face or contact exists in records but neither member exposes it. | More work needed on subject exposure. This is not proof of concealed defective geometry. |
| Both members show the same broken relationship. | The street remains unresolved in both phases. |
| The selected subject, held camera, actor snapshot, detail availability, weather or lens changes between members. | Invalid control. No phase attribution follows. |

The kerb episode makes the evidence limit concrete: **an unreadable face is
not yet a demonstrated bad face**. The crowd episode makes the visual failure
concrete: **a readable fence of bodies is a demonstrated failed human
silhouette**, even when the instance and draw records are correct. Neither
can be converted into a pass because the night member looks appealing.

## Acceptance audit and the missing safeguards

The existing ribbon/marker disqualifier already rejects the crowd failure,
including a separated field of identical uprights. I did not add another
version or weaken it into a ban on all aligned people. A readable queue can
be aligned; a row that reads as street construction still fails.

The remaining gap was **exposure of the actual subject**. Emission identity,
frustum membership and residency can all be true while the camera does not
show the face or contact necessary to judge it. The hero specification now
requires actual emitted foreground surfaces to be associated with their
owning chunks, named in provenance and exposed at the intended viewing size.
It explicitly disqualifies a required subject that is too distant, hidden,
seen from an uninformative side or represented only by its upper surface
when a front, return or contact is required.

It also disqualifies **surface graphics replacing physical street grammar**:
conspicuous hatch, paint or glow without readable walking support, boundary
and body-to-ground relationships. Contrast is not an approval mechanism.
Material detail must explain form rather than stand in for it.

**The hero and the kerb diagnostic retain separate jobs.** The hero must
explain its counter-front walking route and the boundary to the adjoining
street. It cannot certify a kerb face it does not expose. If a raised kerb
is claimed as evidence, its top, return and road joint must be identifiable;
otherwise that surface judgement stays with the dedicated diagnostic.
An authored flush transition may qualify through a clear material boundary
and its relation to the route; the criterion does not demand an invented step.
Do not move the hero into the road or above the stalls to make it perform
that inspection. No extra hero angle or relocated actor can repair a required
relationship missing from the prescribed composition.

These are acceptance and evidence requirements, not a new pose algorithm,
surface redesign or implementation review. The selection remains the
earliest complete right-hand emitted stall run downstream of the live spine
origin, viewed from its approach gap along the counter-front strip to its exit.

## Scene notes — later-stage vocabulary only

These observations are seed material for later judgement under
[D26](DECISIONS.md#d26--the-crowd-is-a-local-simulation-and-the-city-stages-scenes).
They introduce no roles, scripts, schema, steering rules or separation-work
requirements. No scene layer is authorised by these notes.

- **A cablecar-deck frame — shared passage and loose company.** Separate
  walkers occupy different lateral positions inside the railing. Neighbouring
  figures form small groups with open deck between them; shoulder and arm
  outlines vary rather than repeating the railing's supports. Their
  arrangement follows the bend. Shared travel is a visual inference from
  grouping, orientation and walking-like posture. The still does not prove
  conversation, yielding or anyone parting around a moving obstacle.
- **B snow-ropeway frame — approach and gathering.** Readable near walkers
  use the stair surface inside its rails; collective occupation becomes
  busier down the commercial avenue between warm edges. The quieter approach
  and gathered destination belong to different parts of the same route.
  The image establishes that spatial organisation. It does not resolve a
  particular transaction, stall queue or individual's browsing action.
- **A walkway frame — company in a constrained passage.** Neighbouring near
  silhouettes remain separate on the steps, with their own ground contacts
  and shadows. The stair's direction and enclosing walls explain passage,
  while adjacent figures suggest companions. The still cannot determine
  whether they are pausing, matching pace or waiting for someone.

The useful vocabulary is **passage, company, approach, gathering and
destination**. Those relationships are visible or explicitly marked as
inference. They contrast with our captured ribbon's single architectural
rhythm and with the idea of a uniform decorative fill.

I cannot substantiate **queueing, browsing, waiting or obstacle avoidance as
actions** from these opened stills. They remain candidate behaviours named
in the brief and D26, not findings smuggled out of that vocabulary. Approach,
dwell, yielding and a transaction need motion evidence. No video was reviewed
in this round. These notes therefore do not pretend to describe everything
the source videos may show.
