## The disqualifier belongs inside D5's list

**The clause already existed in D5 as an appended paragraph.** The brief's
older list omitted it, while [the previous amendment](VISION_ROUND5.md) and
the derived hero specification contained it. I have now put it directly into
[D5's disqualifier list](VISION_ROUND1.md), alongside the other failures, and
made repeated stance explicit. The same clause appears in
[HERO_FRAME_SPEC.md](HERO_FRAME_SPEC.md):

> **People read as street construction or repeated upright markers:** the
> bodies merge into a continuous ribbon or repeat the same upright stance in
> a fence-like row or mechanical field. Their collective silhouette reads as
> an edge, support rhythm or arranged stock rather than independent people
> occupying and using the selected walking strip. This fails even when heads,
> clothing panels and feet can be identified, and the bodies are drawn, lit
> and continued into depth. Judge at the intended whole-frame viewing size;
> identifying the meshes in a magnified crop cannot approve the frame.

This rejects the image's mechanical marker read, rather than banning every
line or shared pose. A purposeful queue can be aligned; waiting bodies can
share a stance. They qualify only when human silhouettes and their relation
to the destination remain readable. Random scatter or cosmetic pose changes
do not repair a route that has no visible occupation or purpose.

## What the daylight/night comparison must discriminate

The specification now binds the comparison to the nearest selected counter's
front and end, the adjoining walking ground and near bodies, the occupied
middle frontage, and the exit opening. These subjects come from the selected
emitted run, frontage corridor and frozen actor snapshot before phase changes.
The reviewer follows the same subjects in each member. There is no licence
to point at a convenient warm patch or a recognisable person elsewhere.

**Daylight must expose construction and use together:** a counter whose
support meets ground, material differences between cloth and solid supports,
independent near people grounded beside it, passage alongside counter-facing
occupation, and that occupation continuing towards the exit.

**Night must preserve those relationships through received light:** warm
pavement remains a walking surface beneath the same people, the corresponding
counter front remains approachable, and successive lit frontage and shadowed
intervals retain the route to the same opening. Bodies may read through lit
clothing or silhouettes against ground. Night can simplify stock detail and
upper-city architecture; it cannot simplify occupants into posts.

The historical daylight market makes the weak frontage and isolated or merged
bodies easy to inspect across a broad grey floor. Its night counterpart has
a muted warm patch on ground while counter fronts and people largely vanish.
That is why the comparison must join source, surface and occupant. The patch
alone answers none of the hero's relationships.

The amended discriminator requires a finding to name the corresponding
subject and the relationship that one phase exposes while the other hides.
It distinguishes:

- **Daylight exposes the defect; night conceals it:** darkness or bloom hides
  the same ribbon, missing support, empty strip or broken continuation. The
  night image's appeal cannot approve the street.
- **Night exposes the defect; daylight conceals it:** stronger modelling of
  form makes an unsupported counter, floating-body read or repeated markers
  apparent. Revisit that subject in daylight rather than soften the revealing
  light to recover an apparent pass.
- **Daylight resolves the relationship; night erases it:** the corresponding
  occupants, counter or ground connection disappear under the night treatment.
  Night lighting needs work; that loss alone does not identify a geometry bug.
- **Both expose a broken relationship:** the street remains unresolved.
- **Both retain the bound relationships:** the street is resolved and light
  is directing attention without concealing the required human scale.

Unjudgeable detail remains an evidence limit. A changed camera, actor snapshot,
detail availability, weather or lens state invalidates the controlled pair.
Neither case is permission to infer a hidden defect or award a visual pass.

## Scene notes for the later stage

For these notes, a **scene** is a readable relationship between people and a
particular place: passage, company or gathering that belongs to its route or
destination. This is a description of visible organisation, not a proposed
script system or an additional separation requirement.

- **Shared passage around a bend — A cablecar-deck frame.** Neighbouring
  walkers read as travelling together within a larger stream. Their grouping
  leaves ground between successive groups; they follow the deck's curve
  inside its rail. Co-travel is a visual inference from proximity, orientation
  and posture. The still cannot prove matched speed or conversation.
- **A quieter approach leading into a busy commercial route — B snow-ropeway
  frame.** Near walkers occupy the stair surface inside the rails. Farther
  down, collective activity fills the avenue between warm commercial edges.
  The place has different kinds of occupied space along its route; people
  are not a uniform decorative fill. Individual browsing and queue order
  cannot be established at that distance.
- **Company on a constrained stair passage — A walkway frame.** Near figures
  stand separately on the steps, with ground contact and cast shadows. Their
  adjacency and shared route suggest companions. The stair and enclosing
  facades make the passage intelligible even with sparse occupation. This
  still does not establish who yields, stops or changes pace.

I cannot identify a legible stall-browsing scene or crossing wait in these
opened frames. Browsing, queueing and waiting remain candidate scenes named
by [D26](DECISIONS.md), rather than findings invented from its vocabulary.
Motion evidence would be needed to establish approach, dwell, yielding or
an actual transaction. These observations are linked from D26 for later
design. Its present scope remains **agents move around without colliding**.
