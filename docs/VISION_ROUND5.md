## The first look

occupants of the walking surface, or as its edge treatment?** Inspect that
relationship in the whole frame at its intended viewing size, before opening
a magnified crop. This is the first subject, not a fresh checklist of the

I am worried about a bright kerb becoming the apparent footing of a dark
picket fence. The existing capture already gives the crowd a continuous

a single piece of street construction. The improved value structure would

attractive and the feet apparently grounded while the occupants still vanish

## Is there a hole in the hero criteria?

**Yes, the disqualifier list needs to name this failure. But the full hero
contract never permitted it.** D5's positive foreground requirement already
asks for heads, shoulders and separated leg shapes to establish human scale.
D7 explicitly rejects a merged dark bar and repetition that reads as fence
posts. The derived hero specification also requires separate bodies in
daylight and preservation of those relationships at night.

The operational hole is that someone could treat D5's named disqualifiers as
an exhaustive approval test and stop after confirming the presence of a
populated aisle, readable counters, received light and continuation into
depth. A bright continuous ribbon could evade that shortened test. It cannot
satisfy the actual silhouette requirement. The amendment puts the failure at
the point where that shortcut would otherwise happen, rather than adding a
new goal after a frame fails.

The added disqualifier in [D5](VISION_ROUND1.md) and
[the hero-frame specification](HERO_FRAME_SPEC.md) is:

> People read as street construction: a continuous ribbon or mechanically
> repeated row or field of upright markers groups visually with the kerb,
> railing or counter frontage instead of resolving as independent people
> occupying ground. This fails at the intended whole-frame viewing size even
> when the bodies are drawn, illuminated and distributed into depth. A
> magnified crop identifying the meshes cannot approve the frame.

**Do not reduce this to “people must not be on a line”.** A queue, a narrow
stair route or walkers following a railing can read as inhabited. Their
alignment can express use of a place. The rejection concerns the image's
grouping of people into an architectural object, not mathematical
collinearity. Conversely, a mechanically repeated marker field can fail after
every agent has been moved off the original line. A coordinate-distribution
check is therefore neither the aesthetic criterion nor its substitute.

## Application to daylight and night

The new disqualifier applies to each member of the same derived pair. Daylight
must resolve independent occupants against the street. Night must preserve
that identity while received warm light gathers attention around the counter
walk. A sharper dark fence against a luminous pavement does not become a
successful night crowd. Neither does darkness hiding the repetitive bodies
that daylight exposes.

The actual question of whether our route holds those relationships still
requires the paired frames. The specification's exposure to this failure can
be decided now, and has been. The controlled scene and live-state derivation
in HERO_FRAME_SPEC remain in force; this amendment does not change the chosen
route or select a more agreeable crowd for it.
