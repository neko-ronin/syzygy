## A number that looked like a contradiction, and was a mislabelled metric

- **The radial transect's peak lift** was **+59.9** from the orchestrator's read and **+52.3**
  from the reviewer's, on the same band, the same two runs, the same rows. A third read
  (raw 8-bit RGB, no text parser, no resampling) reproduces both **exactly** and shows why:
  they are different metrics of the same pixels. Rec.709 luma gives 73.0 → 132.9 (+59.9);
  the plain RGB mean gives 77.5 → 129.7 (+52.3). PART 18's table says "luma" but its column
  is the RGB mean. Warm pixels are red-heavy, so the plain mean under-weights them and
  compresses the lift by 7.6 luma.
- **Neither measurement is wrong; the label was.** Both agree on shape (a monotone falloff
  from +60 at the lamp row to +18 at the far deck), on the attribution (the lamp is the
  dominant cause, not 15-25 %), and on the verdict. But two people quoting "luma" for a
  strip that changed colour will disagree by several points every time, so the metric has to
  be named. PART 18 carries an annotation to that effect; its numbers are untouched.
- **id124's 2-px bay** counts as a 5th crown in PART 14 and as a 1 in PART 15. Both are
  defensible on their own frames, but a count standard that moves between parts cannot be
  compared across them. If the row is ever re-opened, fix the standard first.

This is a **contract change** and is treated as one: the class list in
`groundgrammar.ts` is load-bearing — the shader compares `surface == 6u` for flood, so indices are

knows a class by number, establish which producer is the smaller honest change (zone-wide ground
mesh vs re-tagging `citygen`'s deck inside the footprint), and — the sentence that decides it —
whether the lamp's pool survives a change to the albedo it is multiplied by, since PART 22's gate
is a **≥ +45 peak lift** on exactly that silt margin.

A gate that refutes the option is a successful gate. Do not start the class round until it lands.

## The gap that outlives these rounds — corrected on 2026-10-22
Three documents (this one, `ROADMAP.md` R11, and PART 20 of
`VISION_FALL_BASELINE.md`) carried the claim that *"section B's reference shows 8-10 window
blocks per crown"*. **That figure was never measured**, and it is a cross-distance
comparison: three of the sheet's four frames are 20-30 m views, and the fourth (top-left) has a
20-30 m shack in the foreground with the far towers behind it. The 8-10 reading comes from that
near foreground. Pose 18 is a 200-270 m skyline. The claim is now replaced by what the frames
actually measure.

Warm-bright marks (R > 120 of 255 and R > 1.6 B), 8-connected, components ≥ 3 px:

```bash
magick <img> -fx "(r>0.47 && r>1.6*b)?1:0" \
  -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=3 -connected-components 8 null:
```

| frame | size | marks | per MP | mean area | mean width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reference, top-left (near shack + far towers) | 520×924 (0.48 MP) | 80 | 167 | 51.2 px² | ~7.2 px |
| reference, bottom-left (the 20-30 m street) | 520×924 (0.48 MP) | 18 | 38 | 141.3 px² | ~11.9 px |
| our pose 18 (`11-09-19`) | 1600×900 (1.44 MP) | **387** | **269** | **6.5 px²** | **~2.5 px** |

So the count is not the deficit — **our frame carries more warm marks per unit area than the
reference's own long view does**. What is missing is the *size of each mark*: ~2.5 px across
against ~7 px (far view) and ~12 px (near view), and normalised by frame width that is a
~15× angular difference (0.16 % of frame width against 2.3 %).

**Caveat, stated because it weakens the size column:** the reference is a JPEG at 520 px wide,
so blur inflates each blob at the threshold and merges close neighbours. The count-per-area
comparison is robust in the direction that matters (JPEG merging would *lower* the reference's
count, and ours is still higher); the size ratio should be read as "at least", not to two
figures.

**What this means for the next round.** Adding blocks to the crowns would produce more specks
at the same 2.5 px and would not move the frame toward the sheet. If the row is ever reopened,
the lever is bigger marks — longer bays, a brighter row, or a nearer pose — not more of them.
`id124`'s 2-px bay is the same problem in its smallest form. The row still closed at 5 of 6 on
PART 15's per-crown count, which is a compositional measure and was not wrong; it is the
*8-10 density target* that was unmeasured.
