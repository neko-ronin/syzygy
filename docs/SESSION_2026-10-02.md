# Session 2026-10-02 — the phantom blocker, and a redirect

Fifty commits. One of them removed a blocker that had cost two sessions, and
most of the rest removed a failure to look at things.

**The headline: there was never a pedestrian rendering bug.** The crowd has
always rendered. 2,550 pedestrians, correct transforms, correct draw, correct
counters — and no lateral spread, so every agent sat at `z = 0.35` on a 1-D
line. A one-dimensional smear of bodies reads as an empty street. Two sessions
opened those frames, correctly saw no *figures*, and reported the crowd as not
rendering. Then two more sessions hunted a winding bug and a culling bug. Six
sessions, every reviewer reading the frames correctly, all of them wrong about
what they were looking at.

That is not a renderer fault and it never was. `F1F2_GPU_HALF.md` records the
four hypotheses that were refuted by measurement, twice each — once against a
CPU model of the pipeline and once against the real one — before a capture
settled it.

## The arc

**Phase 1 — clearing the decks.** The street field was not a distance field:
it evaluated the domain warp at the query point, so `out.dist` was the distance
to a line whose position depended on where you asked from. Measured against a
bisected line, error was `p50 0.71m / max 20.12m`; after the fix, `max 0.001m`.
Every building's setback derives from that number, so the whole city stood
further back from its carriageways than its own footprints implied, and R11
places its lots off the same field.

The crowd's two counters disagreed because they were two scans of one
population with movement between them — Pass B counted against a pre-step
position, `buildInstances` re-tested the post-step position. The instance record
is now written where the count happens, so the disagreement is structurally
impossible rather than merely rare.

The pavement "hold" turned out to be a teleport: it displaced the median agent
**24.12 m in a single frame**, and did so identically at `dt=0` and `dt=1/15` —
a 0.2% spread across a 60-fold change in dt. It is now a clamped rate.

`F4` (traffic) was found to have been implemented a session earlier while the
document still listed it as open work. It was gating R1's second sub-problem.

**Phase 2 — the redirect.** Six rounds went into a standoff between
path-following and a lane hold, balancing to 0.0002 m/frame with the hold
straining at exactly `HOLD_SPEED * dt` on every agent every frame. The mechanism
was fully explained and no fix shipped. The owner redirected it:

> part Lenia, part boids, part Hollywood scene making with Extras. The city has
> scenes and certain sections emit a script an NPC picks up and acts out.

`DECISIONS.md` **D26** records that, supersedes D25, and notes the thing that
had been carried wrong: **the recorded refutation of avoidance was measured on a
degenerate population** — every agent on a line, nearest-neighbour p50
0.030 m. Nobody had ever measured avoidance in this project. "Get them moving
without collisions" was the first honest attempt at R12.2, not a demotion.

**Phase 3 — what the instruments were doing.** Two separate faults turned out to
be faults *in the measuring*, not in the thing measured:

- The crowd's neighbour buffer contained **no near neighbour for 100% of
  agents** — nearest in buffer 3.396 m against a true nearest of 0.000 m, a
  90.7x ratio. `cellRange` scans whole 8 m cells in cell order and at ~14
  agents/m² one cell holds ~900 bodies. Separation had been steering bodies
  apart from strangers, which is why giving it authority made overlap *worse*.
- Three rounds of "the kerb is missing" were all **the artifact, never the
  surface**: first the pose pointed at the world origin, then the corrected
  derived pose stood 20 m too far, then the crowd standoff made the street read
  flat. The measurement was right each time; the thing being photographed was
  wrong.

The kerb itself was authored at **4.319x contrast** with nothing consuming it —
computed, stored in vertex colour, discarded at the fragment stage. The street
was not unlit, it was unshaded. With the consumer restored and the pose corrected,
the face reads at 18–37 px instead of 0.45 px.

## The drift, named honestly

`PROCESS.md` rule 10 sets the goal: *iterate until the output reaches near or
surpasses the reference*. **This session produced fifty commits and no frame that
anyone judged better than the baseline.** Every gate returned
`more work needed`.

The failure was not that the work was wrong — most of it was correct, measured
fixes. The failure was that I kept rewarding **diagnosis** because diagnosis
produces numbers, which is the same substitution the previous session made with
invented thresholds, wearing better clothes. The kerb fix was right and
invisible. The street SDF was right and invisible. **A measured fix nobody has
photographed is not yet progress.**

Two corrections followed, and they are in the documents rather than in a memory:

- **A gate verdict is the output, not a commit.** `DESIGN.md` §6 already said no
  task closes without a vision verdict; this session is what it looks like when
  that is ignored for a day.
- **Change one thing, then photograph it.** Both the crowd and the kerb
  sequences stacked changes and then tried to read the result.

**A note on how these numbers were obtained, because the first attempt was
wrong.** The initial run was `./node_modules/.bin/tsc --noEmit 2>&1 | tail -5;
echo "TSC_EXIT=$?"`, and it printed `TSC_EXIT=0`. That `$?` is **`tail`'s**
exit code, not `tsc`'s — so the "clean" result was the exit status of the tool
that printed the errors. It happened to agree with the truth, which is the
worst possible outcome: a check that reports success for the wrong reason.

`PROCESS.md` rule 9 says *success is not evidence of correctness* and names this
exact shape. Re-run without the pipe, every check above is exit 0 with no
output. **A verification whose exit code comes from a pipeline is not a
verification.**

**Two checks are red on purpose and must stay red until they are fixed:**

## Platform

16 GB / 8 cores, shared with at least two other workspaces of agents. Peak load
**158 on 8 cores** with six `opencode` processes at ~1 GB each. Free memory
swung between **67 MB and 1.3 GB within minutes**, which is why capture slots
were rationed and an agent polled for them rather than waiting to be scheduled.

`tools/orchestrate.mjs` was built for this: a preflight that refuses to start a
capture when the box cannot produce a frame worth believing, an exclusive GPU
lock that reaps itself, and a pane watcher. Its one real bug — swap judged as
an absolute rather than a ratio — is fixed.

**Disk is the unaddressed risk: 14.4 GB free and falling, and it is not this
repo.** `shots/` is 90 MB. Closing agents fixed RAM; nothing has fixed disk.
