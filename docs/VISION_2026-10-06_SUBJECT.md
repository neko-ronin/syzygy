## Frame verdict

In daylight the nearby counter and canopy give the right edge a real market
subject. The support reaches down through the foreground, the top/end are
distinct planes, and successive canopy edges extend into the middle distance.
Counter goods read as a few large plain blocks. The front below the top is
mostly a flat dark area, without enough visible working construction to make
this a finished stall.

Orange, brown and grey upright forms occupy the foreground and repeat along
the aisle. The nearest forms have block heads and broad rectangular torsos
with thin dark lower members. At whole-frame size they read as similarly posed
markers, with shoulders, independent limbs and grounded feet insufficiently
separated. Their repeated vertical rhythm merges with the canopy posts. The
view contains occupation, but it does not yet read as people passing and
using the counter along an inhabited route. These stills cannot judge walking
motion, avoidance or body separation over time.

The foreground and passing corridor remain a broad, smooth pale-grey plane.
A brown boundary line bends toward the middle distance, but the ground does
not give a convincing paving scale or make footway/carriageway construction
clear. The isolated line is not the raised kerb's required top, face, adjoining
higher pavement and lower road in one readable relationship.

At night, orange upper-body patches survive farther left and through the
middle, and a warm pool illuminates the left ground. The near counter front,
canopy underside, support contact and closest bodies become near-black. The
floor pool does not continue onto enough adjoining working surfaces and human
forms to explain the selected route. The daylight control already fails the
finished construction/inhabitation condition: night hides more of that same
unresolved street. It does not demonstrate phase destroying an otherwise
approved daylight street, and this review does not select a shader cause.
